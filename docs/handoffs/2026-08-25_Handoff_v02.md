### The bug worth remembering

The engine was granting Quebec the payroll contribution credits **and** the
deduction for workers. Quebec replaced those credits with that deduction, and
Revenu Québec's own annual formula has no QPP, EI or QPIP credit term at all,
so the model double counted about 565 dollars of relief at 60,000 of income.

**Every test passed before the fix.** The tests and the code shared the same
wrong assumption. It surfaced only because the result was checked by hand
against the published formula: 45,328.53 by hand against 45,893.08 from the
model.

Fixing it changed a conclusion, not just a number: Quebec has the highest
effective deduction rate of any jurisdiction at high income, which the bug had
been masking. Checking one full result by hand is now a documented required
step in `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/ANNUAL_UPDATE.md`.

### Findings

1. **Province choice dominates the conversion basis.** At 300,000 the Austria
   over Canada ratio spans 0.906 in Nunavut to 1.048 in Nova Scotia. The FX
   versus PPP toggle moves it about two points. Austria is behind in the
   territories and Alberta, and ahead across most of Atlantic Canada at every
   income in the range.
2. **Austrian employers carry roughly four times the Canadian load.** 29.6% of
   salary flat to 100,000, against 7.7% falling to 2.1% as every Canadian
   ceiling binds by about 85,000. At a 100,000 CAD package the Austrian
   employer pays about 22% more for the same employee purchasing power. That is
   a large part of why Austrian nominal salaries sit lower.
3. **Revenu Québec truncates its published bracket constants where CRA rounds.**
   Its top constant computes to 10,465.54 and is published as 10,465.

### Known limitations, all documented in the app

- Single taxpayer, no dependants, employment income only.
- Provincial employer health taxes are excluded: they depend on an employer's
  total payroll rather than on one salary, so they cannot be attributed to a
  single job. Vienna's transport levy is excluded for the same reason.
- No housing adjustment, and no value attributed to what social insurance buys.
- PPP reference year is 2025 while the tax year is 2026.
- One tax year of data, so no year-over-year comparison yet.

## Risks or decisions needed

- **#18 housing-adjusted mode** is probably the largest remaining improvement to
  realism, since housing dominates a Vancouver against Vienna comparison and
  national PPP mutes it. It needs a citable rent dataset. Crowd-sourced
  cost-of-living sites do not meet the provenance bar set in the ADR, so this
  needs a sourcing decision first.
- **#19 value of deductions** is the hardest to source defensibly, since
  pension entitlement value depends on career length, retirement age and
  discount rate, none of which the single-year model holds.
- **#22 couples and dependants** would move the model from per-taxpayer to
  per-household, which touches the whole engine. Worth an ADR before starting.

### Tech debt

Test failures cascade in four of the five UI suites. A test that fails part way
never reaches its own `root.unmount()`, and the root it leaves mounted makes
every later test in that file fail too, burying the real failure. This cost
debugging time three times in one session. `App.test.tsx` is fixed with an
`afterEach`; `Charts`, `AuditView`, `Methodology` and `AssumptionsPanel` are
not. A shared render helper would fix all of them in one small change.

### Tooling traps, both recorded in `docs/ANNUAL_UPDATE.md`

- **canada.ca blocks scripted fetches** on their TLS fingerprint and resolves to
  IPv6 first. Use `tools/source.sh`.
- **`vite preview` cannot serve the app for a screenshot**: it returns 404 for
  the module script when the request carries CORS headers. `tools/screenshot.sh`
  uses a plain static server instead.
