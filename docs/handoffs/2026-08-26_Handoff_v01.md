### What 2025 cost, and why it was not just more data

Four things changed part way through 2025, and each has two correct-looking
numbers: an **annual** figure, which the tax return uses and which this model
wants, and a **prorated payroll** figure, which exists only to work off what was
withheld under the old rule.

| Change | Annual 2025 | Prorated for payroll |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Federal lowest rate | 14.5% | 14% from July |
| Manitoba basic personal amount | 15,780 | 15,591 from July |
| Alberta lowest rate | 8% | 6% from July |
| Nova Scotia basic personal amount | 11,744 flat | tested, then a corrective formula |

Taking the prorated figure would have been wrong by a visible margin every time.
The July edition of T4127 states both and says which is which. This is now
written into `docs/ANNUAL_UPDATE.md`.

The issue expected Nova Scotia's income-tested basic personal amount to still
apply in 2025 and so exercise the phase-out shape. It does not: the announcement
of 2025-02-18 set it at the maximum for the whole of that year.

### The Manitoba finding

CRA publishes Manitoba's thresholds twice and inconsistently. T4127 and T4032MB
both give 47,000 and 100,000 with a basic personal amount of 15,780; the "tax
rates and income brackets" page gives 47,564 and 101,200.

The payroll publications are right. Manitoba stopped indexing on 2025-03-20,
47,564 is exactly 47,000 indexed by the 1.2% rate used before the freeze, and
the published bracket constant settles it: 47,000 x 0.0195 = 916.50, published
as 917, where 47,564 gives 927.50.

Same shape as the British Columbia 5.6% typo. **Two of these now. When a CRA
page disagrees with T4127, believe T4127 and check the constants.**

### The hand check earned its keep again

The first British Columbia working for 2025 gave 5,437.64 of provincial tax
against the engine's 5,447.89. The engine was right: taxable income of 98,926
lands 366 above BC's second threshold at 98,560, so a third band applies that
the arithmetic had skipped. Ten dollars, and it would have looked like agreement
at a glance. The near-miss is now a comment in the test.

## Risks or decisions needed

- **#51 needs a deflator decision if you want option B.** Comparing years in
  real terms means picking an index, and CPI is not the only candidate: each tax
  authority indexes by its own published factor, and Austria's is two thirds of
  its inflation rate by statute. That is a modelling choice on the same footing
  as the PPP basis, so it deserves an ADR alongside
  `docs/decisions/2026-08-24_ppp-basis.md`.
- **The conversion default differs in kind between years, deliberately.** 2026
  is not over, so its exchange rate default is a daily reference rate; 2025 is
  complete, so its default is the ECB annual average. Both are user inputs and
  both are dated. When 2026 closes, its default should become the annual average
  too, and `docs/ANNUAL_UPDATE.md` does not yet say so.
- **Manitoba (#46) is closed but worth re-checking each year.** If the province
  resumes indexation, the CRA rates page becomes right by accident. The
  instruction recorded is to confirm against T4032MB rather than assume the
  disagreement persists.

### Structural notes

Two things moved so that adding a year stays a data change:

1. Parameter interfaces now live in `src/data/types.ts`, matching the existing
   `provinces/types.ts` pattern. Before this, the engine imported its shapes
   from `canada-federal-2026.ts` and friends, which would have forced the 2025
   files to import their types from the 2026 files.
2. `canada-2026.ts` became `canada.ts` and takes an optional year.

`src/engine/` needed no change for the second year, which is the property the
registry exists to protect.
