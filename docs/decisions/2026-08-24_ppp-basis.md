# PPP basis: household consumption by default, GDP PPP as an alternate

## Status

Accepted

## Context

The comparison converts net income in two currencies onto a common scale. Two
conversion bases are meaningful, and they can disagree substantially:

- **FX.** The market exchange rate. Answers "what is this worth if I move the
  money", which matters for savings, remittances and debts held abroad.
- **PPP.** A price-level adjustment. Answers "what can I buy where I live",
  which is the stated objective of the project.

Within PPP there is a further choice. The predecessor workbook used GDP PPP. GDP
PPP is built from the whole basket of final expenditure: household consumption,
but also government consumption, gross capital formation and net exports.
Household final consumption expenditure PPP is built from the consumer basket
alone.

## Decision

Offer both FX and PPP as comparison bases, user-selectable.

Within PPP, default to **household final consumption expenditure PPP**, and
offer **GDP PPP** as a selectable alternate.

FX is a user input with a sourced, date-stamped default, not a live fetch. There
is no backend, and a hardcoded rate would go stale within days.

Every conversion factor carries its source and reference year, shown in the
audit view.

## Consequences

Positive:

- The default answers the question the project actually asks. A person's
  purchasing power is governed by consumer prices, not by the price level of
  government consumption or capital formation.
- Keeping GDP PPP available preserves continuity with the workbook's results, so
  a difference between the two is visible rather than silently absorbed.

Negative:

- Two PPP series to source and keep current instead of one.
- Neither series corrects for the largest real divergence between Vancouver and
  Vienna, which is housing. National PPP mutes sub-national price variation
  badly. This is a documented limitation in the methodology page and a backlog
  item, not something the PPP toggle solves.
- PPP series are revised, and are published with a lag. The reference year will
  usually trail the tax year, which the audit view must state rather than hide.
